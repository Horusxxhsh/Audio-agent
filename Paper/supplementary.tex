\section{Evidence Sources and Metric Definitions}

All results in this document are computed from the same evaluation pipeline and data as the main manuscript: Protocol-A top-1 retrieval, Protocol-B EPR projection, split audit, mechanism ablations, dimensionality-matched control, cross-split stability, EPR sensitivity and validity audits, Protocol-C degraded-input results, and the listening study. Metrics follow the main-paper definitions: \textbf{Norm.L2} is RMSE over flattened numeric parameter leaves after plugin-range normalization; \textbf{Norm.Acc@0.1} is the fraction of normalized numeric leaves within tolerance $0.1$; \textbf{Recall} measures retrieval over active numeric parameters; \textbf{Cosine} is cosine similarity in the flattened parameter space; and \textbf{SwitchF1} is the value-aware active-switch F1. The key-presence \textbf{Module} Jaccard is reported only as a diagnostic because it can reward complete-schema outputs without verifying switch values. Missing numeric keys are treated as zeros and non-numeric leaves are ignored.

\section{Protocol-A: Split Audit and Benchmark Definition}

The external benchmark corpus contains 1267 records in total. A post-hoc audit of the original song-name-based held-out list revealed exact cross-split audio reuse, so the manuscript anchors its main objective evidence to a stricter resolved-audio-grouped split: the song-name split has 211 test queries / 1056 knowledge-base items with 16 shared audio paths and 48 near-duplicate pairs under a lightweight audio-fingerprint check, whereas the resolved-audio grouped split has 204 / 1063 with no shared paths (200 near-duplicate pairs remain, indicating a risk surface rather than a formal perceptual-duplicate guarantee). The stricter split groups records by their underlying audio source file before sampling the held-out set; it contains 204 paired TRR rows but only 203 unique \texttt{query\_name} labels because \texttt{Hard Rock Crunch} appears twice; all pairing therefore uses \texttt{query\_idx}.

\section{Unified Protocol-A/Protocol-B Main Comparison}

Protocol-A compares top-1 executable retrieval on a shared 204-query split and a 1,063-record retrieval database; Protocol-B applies EPR-K5 projection over the same retrieved neighborhoods. Coverage was verified before admission: TRR and PaSST are full coverage (204/204 queries, 1063/1063 candidates; PaSST embeddings were recomputed for this study), Wav2Vec and FeatureNN cover all queries with 1058/1063 candidates, CLAP has partial coverage (173/204, 829/1063), and PANNs is excluded because its model weights could not be obtained in our evaluation environment. Under this protocol, TRR achieves the strongest full-coverage top-1 retrieval results, and TRR EPR-K5 is the best projection row (Norm.L2 0.1334, SwitchF1 0.8824); the range-projected MLP matches TRR on Norm.Acc@0.1 but is weaker on Recall (0.3468) and Cosine (0.5941).

\section{TRR Mechanism Ablations}

The mechanism audit includes one backbone-controlled comparison: TRR and Wav2Vec share the Wav2Vec2 backbone and differ only in the aggregation operator (projected frame activations followed by Gram second-order statistics and L2 normalization versus first-order mean pooling). Under Protocol-A, the Gram representation improves normalized L2 from 0.2400 to 0.1881 while the backbone is held fixed (Norm.Acc@0.1 0.5015$\rightarrow$0.5766).

To further test the mechanism, we recomputed controlled Wav2Vec2 layer features on the same 204-query Protocol-A split with a fixed random seed and deterministic projection matrices. Table~\ref{tab:trr_mechanism_ablation} reports same-backbone aggregation variants and supports four bounded claims. First, Gram aggregation is not merely a backbone effect: same-layer Gram variants improve over same-backbone mean pooling. Second, the gain is strongest at a single mid-level layer (layer~5) rather than from multi-layer averaging: mean-pooling across layers~4--6 improves only marginally over layer~5 alone (0.2552 vs.\ 0.2582), and multi-layer Gram with $d=64$ improves only marginally over multi-layer mean pooling (0.2546 vs.\ 0.2552), so the reported TRR advantage is not experimentally isolated from the choice of layer~5. Third, L2 normalization is important: removing it produces the weakest row. Fourth, architectural choices remain meaningful: layer~5 and $d=32$/$d=64$ multi-layer variants trade off different metrics, so the reported configuration is not globally optimal.

\subsection{Dimensionality-Matched Control Baseline}

To rule out the ``higher dimensionality'' explanation for the TRR advantage (4096-D Gram descriptor versus 768-D mean-pooled baseline), we project the 768-D Wav2Vec vector to 4096-D via a fixed random Gaussian projection (seed 42, elements $\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1/\sqrt{768})$) followed by L2 normalization. The control reaches Norm.L2 $=0.1999$, statistically tied with the 768-D baseline ($0.1997$) and far above TRR ($0.1454$), confirming that the advantage cannot be attributed to higher descriptor dimensionality alone.

\subsection{Cross-Split Stability}

To verify that the main result is not an artifact of a single random split, we construct five independent splits under the same resolved-audio-grouping protocol with different random seeds. Table~\ref{tab:split_stability} shows that TRR achieves the lowest Norm.L2 on all five splits, with a mean of $0.1500 \pm 0.0032$; the Wav2Vec baseline varies more ($0.1904 \pm 0.0087$) and never approaches the TRR level.

\section{Protocol-C: Robustness to Realistic Degradations}

Protocol-C corrupts raw audio (AWGN, MP3 compression, reverberation, truncation) and re-encodes the degraded signal into TRR embeddings, using \texttt{Style}+\texttt{Feature} as the text branch and parameter-space oracle neighbors as relevance targets. Table~\ref{tab:protocolC_summary} shows that adaptive fusion is the best overall setting (L2 $=20.1491$, Acc@0.1 $=0.6121$, MRR $=0.5654$, NDCG@5 $=0.5566$) but with a small margin over strong fixed text-weighted fusion. Per-condition, adaptive fusion is not a uniform winner: fixed 0.50 and fixed 0.70 remain competitive on several conditions. The stronger conclusion is that audio-heavy fusion is fragile, whereas adaptive fusion consistently moves the system toward the stable mid-to-high text-weight region. An independent boundary analysis with explicit audio-only ($\alpha=0.0$), pure-text ($\alpha=1.0$), and parameter-oracle upper-bound rows confirms that audio-only retrieval is weak under realistic degradation, mid/high text weights are stable, and the pure-text boundary remains a strong practical boundary (best L2 $=61.5079$); the oracle row quantifies only the remaining improvement attainable with perfect retrieval. Thus, adaptive fusion should be read as a multimodal operating strategy near the stable region, not as proof of superiority over text-only retrieval.

\section{Listening Study Statistics}

The listening study uses a multiple-stimulus protocol with a hidden reference and no explicit low-quality anchor; across all trials it contains 26 unique participants and 910 ratings (Trial 1: 208 ratings across 8 stimuli; Trials 2--5: 312; Trials 6--10: 390). The raw logs preserve repeated-measures structure and participant IDs, but not device metadata, participant expertise labels, or the manual-baseline time budget; we therefore interpret the results as perceptual context rather than as a fairness-critical human-vs-system benchmark. Table~\ref{tab:listening_tests} reports repeated-measures nonparametric tests: in Trials~2--5 the TRR-based system and the manual baseline both trail the reference ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.00015$), with the TRR-based system rated above the manual baseline; in Trials~6--10 the TRR-based system and MusicGen are rated comparably ($p=0.7797$).

\section{CLAP-Covered Shared Subset}

CLAP coverage is partial in the released dataset, so we recompute a matched comparison on the intersection where Wav2Vec, FeatureNN, CLAP, PaSST, and TRR all have vectors for both queries and candidates (173 queries, 829 candidates). On this shared subset, TRR remains best on top-1 Norm.L2 (0.1487), PNR@5 (0.8150), and EPR-K5 Norm.L2 (0.1328); the best baseline (PaSST) reaches 0.1894 on top-1.

\section{Uncached Latency Audit}

The cached latency table in the main text measures retrieval scoring after embeddings are already available. An examination of the released code found no verified end-to-end live path that jointly performs audio preprocessing, Wav2Vec2 forward inference, TRR re-encoding, search, and validation for newly supplied reference audio. The paper therefore uses the cached latency table only as a retrieval-scoring diagnostic and does not claim end-to-end live reference-audio latency.

\section{Protocol-B Executable-Neighborhood Diagnostics}

Protocol-B evaluates whether retrieval produces a parameter-transferable executable neighborhood rather than only a single nearest preset. \textbf{PNR@K} is parameter-neighborhood recall at threshold $0.10$ over the retrieved set; EPR denotes a softmax-weighted blend over the retrieved executable presets followed by range and switch projection, with provenance reported as effective exemplars and maximum exemplar weight. Table~\ref{tab:executable_neighborhood_supp} reports the neighborhood diagnostics: the primary hybrid row is EPR-K5 (Norm.L2 $=0.1334$, SwitchF1 $=0.8824$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}}=4.2129$, $\max_i w_i=0.3438$), which improves over top-1 retrieval while retaining provenance. The range-projected MLP row (Norm.L2 $=0.1603$, Norm.Acc@0.1 $=0.7894$, Recall $0.3468$, Cosine $0.5941$) has no retrieved-neighborhood provenance; its low recall and cosine indicate that direct regression does not recover the same active parameter structure as retrieval, and it does not emit discrete switch states (SwitchF1 is undefined).

\newpage

The key-presence Module Jaccard is not used as a primary conclusion because it can become artificially high for methods that emit a complete parameter schema. Module-aware reranking is included only as a diagnostic negative result: it worsens Norm.L2 (0.1479) and degrades recall and cosine.

Table~\ref{tab:supp_epr_sensitivity} reports EPR sensitivity under softmax weighting (the EPR sensitivity CSV does not contain uniform-weighting runs); $K=5$, $\tau=0.05$ is the primary manuscript row. Table~\ref{tab:supp_epr_validity} reports the validity and repair audit: all methods reach 100\% validity after range and switch projection, and TRR requires repair on 78.4\% of queries (range 43.6\%, switch 78.4\%).
